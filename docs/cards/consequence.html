Pre-demo setup

From cold to ready. Four commands on a Mac, two in a Codespace, about 20 minutes.

#Run thisWhat it does
1 Once per machine
brew install kind helm make dns-setup
 Points your OS resolver at the lab's DNS so *.uma.lab works in a browser. One sudo. Skip both in a Codespace — it uses /etc/hosts instead.
2 Build the lab
make kind-up
 Three-node kind cluster, Istio ambient, kgateway, cert-manager, CloudNativePG, and every party in its own namespace. ~13 minutes cold. If the compose stack is running it will stop you — both want :443 and :53, so run make down and try again.
3 Trust the CA
make k8s-trust-ca sudo security add-trusted-cert -d -r trustRoot \ -k /Library/Keychains/System.keychain /tmp/u4a-k8s-ca.pem
 cert-manager issues the lab CA inside the cluster; this trusts it locally so her portal loads with no warning. Re-run after every kind-up — a new cluster means a new CA. In a Codespace run make codespaces-web instead.
4 Bring up the agent
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-... make kagent
 kagent's controller, the U4A adapter in Bob's namespace, and a model for the agent to think with. Your key goes into a Kubernetes Secret and nowhere else. See Switching the model below.

Open https://portal.uma.lab on the right and log in as alice / alice-demo.

In a Codespace

Only steps 2 and 4. No resolver to edit, no CA to trust — kind-up publishes her portal for you.

The run-through

The agent is stock kagent. It never learns what her rule says — it only ever sees a tool call that is slow, or one that fails. Everything that changes happens on the right screen.

#Do thisSay this
0 Her portal Settings → Security → Agent Authorization Open Trade execution and look at Ask me every time. It is on because somebody once decided this tool was the dangerous one. A list of tool names, kept by hand, that says nothing about why.
1 Terminal — tier 1
make kagent-ask Q="What is in Alice's portfolio?" SIM=0
 Let the agent in first, so the later beats are about her rule and not about first contact.
2 Her portal — badge shows 1 Approve Held for her. She has never met this agent. The holdings come back on the left.
3 Terminal — tier 3
make kagent-ask Q="Sell 200 shares of her AAPL position." SIM=0
 Now ask it to sell something. It stops, because that toggle is on.
4 Her portal read the card · Deny Refused. Read the pending card before denying. It names the tier and what the agent promised. It does not say what the act would do, because nothing in her policy knows.
5 Her portal toggle off · add rule · Save changes The rule is if it cannot be undone at all — ask me first. Trade execution now has no rule naming it. Nothing about the vault changed, and the agent was never told.
6 Terminal — tier 1 again
make kagent-ask Q="What is in Alice's portfolio?" SIM=0
 Straight through, and the portal never moves. Reading holdings is published as reversible, so a rule about what cannot be undone has nothing to say about it.
7 Terminal — tier 3 again
make kagent-ask Q="Sell 200 shares of her AAPL position." SIM=0
 Ask it to sell again. It stops again.
8 Her portal read Why you · Approve The card now says it cannot be undone at all. Nobody put the trade endpoint on a list. The vault publishes that the act is irreversible, her authority pulled that in, and the rule from step 5 read it.
9 Her portal add the same condition · grant without asking Her authority refuses to save it. What an act costs can raise what a request needs and never lower it, so the one rule she cannot write is the one that waves an irreversible act through.
10 Terminal
make k8s-audit
 Her record carries the class beside the tier and the operation, so what was at stake is answerable later without looking up what execute_trade does.
What to point at

The rule names no tool, no agent and no operator. The vault could add a fourth endpoint tomorrow and, if it publishes that the endpoint cannot be undone, her rule covers it the first time an agent asks — with no edit on her side.

If someone asks: who decides the class?

The resource server, in the metadata it signs. It is the party that would have to undo the act. An agent's description of itself stays display-only for the opposite reason.

If someone asks: is this a risk score?

No. The classes order by how much remedy remains, not by how bad the outcome is, and nothing here adds them up.
